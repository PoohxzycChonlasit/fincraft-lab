## Cross-reference conclusions

1. Tactility is strongest when it marks controls, selection, and immediate cause/effect—not when every container floats.
2. Editorial authority comes from ordered claims, readable measures, and explicit sources, not luxury finance imagery.
3. A local knowledge view answers learning questions better than a visually impressive all-node map.
4. Share imagery must remain a derivative communication artifact; it cannot replace semantic HTML or educational context.
5. The most distinctive FinCraft direction combines Kigen-like control discipline, reading-first editorial hierarchy, and local-first knowledge navigation while refusing the dominant dark SaaS/gallery aesthetic.

## Rejected reference patterns

- Dark-first finance luxury, trading urgency, or investment-service authority.
- Full-screen animated gradients, video backgrounds, liquid glass, particles, or 3D.
- Continuous force simulation and animated edges.
- Every section rendered as a floating card or pill.
- Gallery masonry as a substitute for product hierarchy.
- Tiny portfolio typography and low-contrast grey controls.
- Image-only educational content or social cards without text alternatives.
- Generic centered SaaS hero, fake metrics, fake activity, or community claims.
- Copying Kigen layouts/components, Obsidian styling/terminology, or third-party brand assets.
